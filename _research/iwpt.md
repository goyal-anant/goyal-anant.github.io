Wireless power transfer between two coils works well only when they sit right on top of each other. Move the receiver a little to the side and the mutual coupling drops, and with it the efficiency. Anyone who has put a phone on a wireless charger slightly off-centre knows this.

Our approach is to put a metasurface between the transmitter and the receiver, which acts like a lens for the magnetic field. It is a three-step process:

1. Design the transmitter and receiver to maximise their mutual coupling.
2. Study how the system behaves under misalignment.
3. Design a lens-like metasurface that restores the coupling when the receiver is misaligned.

In one example, the power transfer efficiency went up by roughly 47 % with the metasurface in place. We also proposed an optimisation approach to find the parameters of the transmitter, the receiver, and the metasurface together.
